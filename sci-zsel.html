Bridging Lexical Divergence: LLM-Assisted, Cost-Efficient, Zero-shot Scientific Entity Linking

Md Rasel Khondokar, Qiao Qiao, Farjana Sultana Samia, Nhat Le, Yuepei Li, Qi Li

Department of Computer Science, Iowa State University

Paper arXiv BibTeX

TL;DR

Scientific entity linking breaks when a mention and its entity share no words. Sci-ZSEL builds training pairs without human labels: it asks an LLM for aliases of a small set of selected entities, drops aliases that drift toward neighboring concepts in the ontology, and uses the rest to fine-tune the linker. It improves every backbone on five benchmarks, with the largest gains on mentions that share no words with their entity.

How Sci-ZSEL works

Instead of calling an LLM once per corpus mention, Sci-ZSEL generates aliases on the entity side for a small set of selected entities. The aliases are then matched against real mentions to form training pairs, and they can be reused for any new corpus that draws on the same ontology.

  1. Entity selection. Pick the ontology entities whose names appear as mentions in the unlabeled corpus, plus each mention's top-1 entity from a pretrained BLINK bi-encoder.
  2. Pseudo-pair construction.My part Generate aliases from each entity's definition with an LLM, filter out aliases that drift to ontology neighbors, and match the remaining aliases to real mentions to build labeled mention–entity pairs.
  3. Ontology-aware retriever fine-tuning. Fine-tune the bi-encoder with hierarchy-aware positives (parents and children) and in-batch negatives.
  4. Reranker fine-tuning. Fine-tune a cross-encoder (BLINK or ReS) on the same pseudo-pairs to rank the retrieved candidates.

My contribution: pseudo-labels

I built the pseudo-labeled data construction and filtering pipeline (Section 5.2 of the paper). It turns an unlabeled corpus and an ontology into mention–entity training pairs, with no human annotation.

Why pseudo-labels are needed

Fine-tuning fixes most of what goes wrong when general-domain linkers are applied to scientific text, but it needs labeled mention–entity pairs. Scientific domains such as animal science have almost none, and expert annotation is expensive. Pseudo-labels supply that training data automatically.

How the pairs are built

Three sources of pairs are combined:

  • Surface-name matching (P1). A mention that exactly matches an entity's name is paired with that entity. Mention ambiguity is low in scientific text, so these pairs are usually correct.
  • LLM-generated aliases (P2). Llama 3.2 3B Instruct reads an entity's definition and lists alternative names. Each alias that survives the filter below is searched for in the corpus; an exact match (after case and whitespace normalization) becomes a pair with the original entity.
  • Ontology synonyms (PSyn). Curated synonyms in the ontology are matched against mentions the same way.

Filtering drifted aliases

LLM aliases often drift: an alias may describe a parent, child or sibling of the entity rather than the entity itself. Each alias is compared with the entity name and with its closest ontology neighbor using cosine similarity of BioLORD embeddings:

s_anchor = sim(name(e), alias)   s_drift = max sim(name(neighbor), alias)

An alias is discarded if s_anchor < s_drift (it is closer to a neighbor than to its own entity) or s_anchor < 0.9 (it has moved too far from the entity name).

What the filter changes

On NCBI Disease and BC5CDR, which have gold labels for their training data, filtering raises pseudo-pair accuracy from about 73% to over 90%:

The ablation shows the filter is necessary: training on unfiltered aliases pushes reranker accuracy below the non-fine-tuned baseline in several settings, with the largest overall Recall@1 drop of 7.50 points on QTL-CMO with ReS. Over 80% of the filtered aliases on the animal science ontologies (bi-encoder top-1 setting) are new names that the ontologies themselves do not list.

A harder benchmark: animal science

The paper releases three expert-curated, test-only datasets built from PubMed articles in AnimalQTLdb, linked to the Clinical Measurement Ontology (CMO), the Vertebrate Trait Ontology (VT) and the Livestock Product Trait Ontology (LPT). Far more of their mentions share no words with their entity, and the ontologies list far fewer synonyms.

Results

Fine-tuning on Sci-ZSEL pseudo-pairs plus curated synonyms improves every backbone on all five datasets. The gains are largest when the mention and entity share no words, up to +49.12 points.
